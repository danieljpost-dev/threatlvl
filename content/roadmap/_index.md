# Roadmap

What is being worked on, and roughly in what order. There are no dates here, and none are implied
— this is a small project built carefully, and the sequence matters more than the schedule.

For per-feature detail, see the [features list](../features).

## Now

**Running a session end to end.** Rooms, membership and roles; characters that live in the engine;
the event log; real-time play over a websocket connection; dice that can be toggled per room or
replaced entirely by physical rolls.

**The combat model beneath all of it.** Re-rooting combat so a creature is one kind of combatant
among several. That unlocks crewed vehicles, resource tracks other than hit points, shared
table-level pools, and zone-based distance — each of which is ordinary in some genre and impossible
to fake cleanly without it.

## Next

**Campaign books.** Ingesting published adventures into a structured form the engine can actually
run: scenes, encounters, read-aloud text, GM-only secrets, loot tables, and the conditional logic
that makes a book branch.

**More systems.** Pathfinder and GURPS on the same foundation as D&D 5e, with per-system and
per-edition licensing handled properly.

## Later

**A marketplace for creators.** Authors publish campaigns, keep their rights, and get paid — both
per sale and by how much their work is actually played. This opens only once the free tier works
without payment and ingestion is solid.

**A GM assistant.** Optional help with monster tactics, scene narration, and eventually per-creature
motivation, so a dragon and a pack of goblins behave differently in the same room.

Every suggestion it makes passes through the GM, who accepts, rejects or rewrites it. Only what the
GM accepts is recorded; rejected suggestions are discarded rather than kept. It is designed so that
it can be switched off entirely, and so that a slow or unavailable model never delays play.

## Things that will not change

- The GM is always in the seat.
- The game stays fully playable with optional subsystems turned off.
- Running a game costs nothing.
